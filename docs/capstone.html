What the instrument measures

Cathedral checks whether operational elements are connected, not whether they're present. Is a threshold bound to an action? A failure mode to a mitigation? A policy to instrumentation? Keyword stuffing — "we have thresholds, metrics, abort conditions, rollback…" — is caught, not rewarded, because binding is what's scored.

Its founding rule cuts in every direction, including at its own maker: never trust surface markers; verify structure. The test the whole program leans on is a word-shuffle control — scramble a document's words and a verdict built on real structure collapses, while one riding on vocabulary survives. Every operational-family verdict reported below was put through twenty seeded shuffles.

The finding, resolved from both sides

Eight registers sit at 0–2%. One stands at 11.6%. The gap is not "planfulness" — Rust RFCs and PEPs are unmistakably plans and stay near the floor. The gap is what the plans bind.

Kubernetes KEPs embed a mandated Production Readiness Review — a written operational plan in every document: how the rollout fails, which metric triggers a rollback, whether the feature can be disabled. Remove just that section from the KEP positives and 53 of 71 drop out, collapsing 11.6% toward the same ~3% as everything else. So the design space is now literal and two-sided: Cathedral credits the written operational plan a document contains — thresholds, failure modes, and responses bound in text — wherever it appears, and stays honestly silent on retrospectives, alert pages, design deliberation, and machine tutorials that carry none.

The change ledger — three fixes shipped, two rejected, in the open

Each fresh register that broke the core honestly forced one change, pinned by regressions on both sides and validated out-of-sample on the next register. Two attempted fixes failed their own pre-registered budgets and were reverted — and published with their numbers, because a rejection with evidence is a result.

v3.23shipped

A repeated signal is not enumeration

Short alert runbooks that bound one signal word many ways survived shuffling. Two guards — signal-side diversity and same-sentence binding — the mirror of rules the core already applied. validated out-of-sample

v3.25shipped

An absolute count is not a plan at any scale

Two bindings among forty-eight signals is co-occurrence, not structure. The reference-length principle extended to the binding path: the count becomes a density past the register it was tuned on. validated out-of-sample

v3.27shipped + rejected

A type/token ratio needs a window, not a scale

The gaming instrument was measuring document length. Windowing it fixed a 15.5%→0.8% false-flag rate. The same run's second fix — a same-sentence binding witness — killed 49 of 56 earned verdicts and was reverted. fix validated witness rejected

A second attempt on the flagship's soft routes, pre-registered with an acceptance budget. It missed on three of four criteria — 40 of 71 earned verdicts lost — and was reverted the same day. Two rejections now fence the design space for whoever tries third. reverted, published

The white-box red team

The strongest realistic attacker: a modern language model with full access to the source and the entire ledger, writing fourteen attacks aimed at named seams — then frozen into git before a single one was run. One shot, no tuning against results.

What stays open — stated plainly

  • The soft boundary is accepted, not solved. A few flagship verdicts on token-dense plans retain 4–9 of 20 shuffles — under every standing threshold, reported by every run, with written conditions that would reopen the question.
  • One face of the authorship flag remains. Ordinary machine text behaves like human text, and a white-box first strike held — but a corpus of genuine operational documents written by an uninformed external model is the one thing this program cannot author for itself. It's the standing invitation.
  • Every corpus here is now development data. The moment a run tunes against a register, that register stops being a test. The next fresh register validates the next change — the pattern that has held for the whole program.

Every figure on this page reproduces. The evaluator is a single dependency-free JavaScript module; each register has a byte-pinned fetch script and a validation script whose gates were committed before its verdicts. Runs are deterministic and were verified identical on re-run. The nine findings documents, the two decision records, and the frozen red-team suite live in the repository under docs/ and validation/.

Cathedral does not measure consciousness or understanding — text is precisely the surface a system can produce with nothing behind it, which is the tool's founding insight, not a limitation it forgot. It measures one thing, honestly, and says so at its edges.

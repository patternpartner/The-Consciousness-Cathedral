# LLM-Runbook Validation — the authorship flag's remaining face, measured in its disclosed inside approximation

*2026-07-17. The flag's pure form — genuine LLM-written operational documents from an uninformed external generator — cannot be produced from inside this project, and this run does not claim to have produced it. What it produced is the approximation pre-registered in [LLM-RUNBOOK-PREREG.md](LLM-RUNBOOK-PREREG.md) (committed `d60849e`, before any text was generated): 120 operational runbooks written by `claude-sonnet-5` in cold generation contexts that had never seen the evaluator, for topics derived mechanically (the alert names of the two byte-pinned human runbook corpora, seeded first 120 of 343) under a frozen prompt template — eliminating the generator-knowledge and prompter-discretion contamination channels, and disclosing the third (model family, environment proximity, instructed-not-proven tool non-use) rather than eliminating it. Generation is stochastic; the committed corpus (`validation/corpus-llm-runbooks.json`, verbatim, zero exclusions, zero re-runs) is the reproducibility object. Evaluation is deterministic given it — verified identical on re-run. Reproduce: `node validation/llm-runbook-validation.js`.*

## The finding

**The elevated-structural reading, pre-stated in the pre-registration, is what happened.** Human runbooks for these same alert names earn the operational family at 1.6% (prometheus-operator) and 2.0% (openshift); the generated runbooks earn it at 4.2% — roughly double — and every positive passes the shuffle control (0/20, 0/20, 0/20, 1/20, 4/20). Per the pre-registered adjudication rule, this is **not an authorship shortcut and not an evaluator defect: the generator writes more bound operational structure than the human corpus contains.** Looking at the five positives (`AlertmanagerFailedReload`, `ODFPersistentVolumeMirrorStatus`, `PrometheusTSDBReloadsFailing`, `SSPDown`, `VirtualMachineStuckInUnhealthyState` — all `OPERATIONAL INTENT`, none flagship), the mechanism is visible in the text: a model writing a runbook "from experience" reliably includes the diagnose→condition→response bindings that many terse human alert pages leave implicit, because it is reconstructing the *genre* of a good runbook rather than paging out one team's minimal notes. The evaluator credits exactly that, structurally, and nothing else — 93 of 120 land in `VERIFIED CONSISTENT`, the same reading human alert runbooks get.

**No authorship signature on any instrument.** Zero gaming flags and zero `OUTSIDE DESIGN SPACE` on 120 machine documents — flatter than any human register (best prior: Cosmopedia at 0.2% gaming). Combined with the v3.30.0 result (machine tutorials, 0.0% family) and the v3.33.0 red-team result (informed adversarial text, nothing minted), the instrument regime's indifference to authorship now stands on three different kinds of machine text.

## Honest limits, restated from the pre-registration

One generator, one model family — the same family as the v3.33.0 white-box attacker (different context, zero evaluator knowledge, but not "LLMs at large"). The generation contexts ran in the same container as this repository: the project *name* was visible in their environment (none of its content), and their tool non-use is instructed and consistent with their zero-tool-use records, not provable. Documents within a batch (15 per context) share a generation context, so style correlates within batches. And 120 documents bound the rates loosely: 4.2% carries a wide interval at this n. **The pure outside ask stands unchanged and is still the most valuable possible contribution: runbooks generated by any model, prompted by someone who has not read this repository.** This run narrows the flag's remaining face from "unmeasured" to "measured under disclosed inside conditions"; it does not close it.
